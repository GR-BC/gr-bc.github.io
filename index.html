Abstract

Judge actions by their consequence, not the trajectory label.

Long-horizon manipulation data is rarely clean. Even successful demonstrations can contain hesitations, overshoots, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. Intervention-assisted rollouts mix autonomous mistakes with delayed expert corrections, while failed episodes can still contain short segments that reconnect the system to a viable path.

GR-BC builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over this mixed-quality data. Reverse graph search estimates the minimal remaining steps to success inside the dataset support, and each physically executed action chunk is weighted by how much it reduces that state potential. New rollouts are appended to the graph and reused in the next offline training round.

The challenge

Trajectory outcome is too coarse for chunk-level credit.

Whether an action is worth imitating depends on what it causes from the current state — not whether the overall episode eventually succeeded or failed.

01

A success can contain regression.

Uniform SFT can clone pauses, detours, and unstable corrections hidden inside a successful episode.

02

A failure can contain recovery.

Discarding the whole rollout wastes valid, task-advancing chunks that reconnect to successful regions.

Approach

One graph. One reverse search. Better supervision.

GR-BC leaves the VLA architecture and supervised objective intact. The graph only decides how strongly each observed action chunk should contribute to training.

A

Build the reachability graph

Multi-view MCR embeddings retrieve candidate neighbors. Mutual-view agreement, a gripper-state hard filter, and locally normalized similarity edges keep connections conservative.

B

Search backward from success

Reverse Dijkstra search to a virtual success sink yields the dataset-supported remaining-step potential Rrec(s), then interpolation makes it dense.

C

Reweight executed chunks

Net progress Δt rewards forward consequence; regression depth filters unstable chunks. Unreachable states receive zero training weight.

p(s)

Graph-Retrieved Progress is a state potential. It is not a per-step reward, value function, Q-function, advantage estimate, or action-retrieval mechanism. GR-BC uses the change in this potential to weight action chunks that were actually executed in the dataset.

Limitations

The signal is only as reliable as the graph.

Incorrect visual neighbors can create shortcut paths; conservative filters can also miss valid recovery bridges. Multi-view agreement and gripper consistency reduce common errors, but they do not certify physical reachability.

GR-BC is intentionally dataset-supported and has limited generalization to states far outside the collected experience. Stronger reachability tests and broader embodiments remain future work.
